## What was measured versus inferred

Printed p41 identifies dried sewage sludge at91.9% dry solids (8.1% residual water), with organic fraction0.556 of dry solids. This is not ash and not the original untreated wet feed. The reviewed pages do not identify the sampled plant, exact drying history, mineral assemblage, or a demonstrated equivalence to the application's target raw sludge. Do not attach the separately analysed digested sludge A/B identities to the calorimeter sample without evidence.

p42:600.0g water equilibrated with calorimeter and200.0g cooled dried sludge; mean initial temperatures20.55±.89°C and5.95±.57°C respectively. Measured *moist dried sample* mean Cp is1.17±.04kJ/(kg K), Figure18 gives mean1.167 and explicitly8.1% water. This is a mixing-calorimetry mean over a finite low-temperature experiment, not a resolved Cp(T) curve or a differential measurement at20°C.

Dry-solids Cp≈.9kJ/(kg K) is **derived** by removing the water contribution using the mixture rule (4-10). With xwater=.081 and ordinary near-room water Cp≈4.18kJ/(kg K), (1.167−.081×4.18)/.919≈.9017, consistent with rounded reported0.90. This consistency calculation is not a new water-property certification or recovery of the unreported raw calorimeter temperatures for every trial.

Table8 p47 labels the experimental dry-solids result0.90±.04 at20°C and organic fraction55.6%. Annex II's title says measured dry-solids Cp, but the method shows these are water-corrected derived values, not independent directly dry-sample measurements. Preserve that distinction in any source schema.

## Uncertainty interpretation

Annex II printed p117 supplies14 derived dry-solids values, mean.8990kJ/(kg K), sample variance.003946, standard deviation.062813 and stated95% confidence interval half-width.0360. These agree at rounding scale with Table8's .90±.04. The ±.04 must not be labelled the standard deviation: Annex II's SD is about.0628. Nor is the mean confidence interval a per-particle/material-range envelope or a bound covering a new sludge batch.

The14 displayed values range.72558–1.00597kJ/(kg K). The normality panel reports Lilliefors p<.05 alongside another K–S p>.20; avoid inventing robust normal-distribution confidence or discarding the low observation. The stated CI is the author's statistical result, not automatically a source-certified worst-case numerical uncertainty.

Calorimeter calibration p41 is.1214±.0124kJ/K; Annex I separately describes its statistics. The reviewed Cp section does not establish a complete propagated uncertainty budget for calibration, temperature readings, masses, residual moisture, sample heterogeneity and water subtraction. Carry these as unquantified systematic/transfer uncertainty; do not replace with zero. The three ± quantities in the prose are not proven to all use the same statistical convention merely because Annex II defines one CI.

## Analytical estimates are a separate model, not extra measurements

pp43–46 analyse two digested sludges using hypothetical organic mixtures of dextrin, palmitic acid and melamine, plus modified ash oxide composition. These substitutions are explicitly approximations: elemental analysis does not determine molecular structure; sulfur/chlorine are neglected at low content; ash sulfate/phosphate representation is changed for data availability. Values1.21 (A) and.94 (B)kJ/(kg K) in Table7 are model estimates; Table8 rounds/reports B as.95. Record this small internal difference rather than silently selecting a preferred value.

The proposed Cp_TS=.79×volatile_fraction+.73 predicts1.16924kJ/(kg K) at the calorimeter sample's .556 fraction, versus its derived mean.8990—a difference~.270kJ/(kg K), about30% of the measured-derived mean. The general correlation is therefore not a fitted reproduction of that actual calorimetry result. Do not use it to convert the sample's organic fraction into a superior uncertainty-free Cp or pool all values as replicate measurements.

## Thermal domain admission

This is a useful actual low-temperature sewage-sludge Cp anchor with composition fraction and repeated observations. It could support a source-linked candidate property record restricted to this dried sample and experiment, explicitly water-corrected and uncertain. It does not identify all species/reference energies needed by current molar total-energy chemistry, and mass-specific Cp cannot determine formation energy, product calorics, latent/reaction enthalpies or mechanical parameters.

It cannot close high-temperature firing/pyrolysis/cooling domain. The source itself says temperature dependence is hard to predict and that even20–60°C can be affected by uncertain organic phase transitions (p48). Table8's20–900°C and25–850°C entries belong to cited external literature, not this thesis's measurements; they require separate primary-source review. No constant0.9 extension to295–310K, much less kiln temperature, is established solely by the near20°C label: the measured interval and unknown transitions must be respected.

Recommended next source action: create a derived-observation record with the14 Annex II values, explicit8.1% water correction, sample organic fraction, statistical fields separated from systematic unknowns and a low-temperature-only scope. Retain source locator/hashes and redistribution restriction. Additional sample identity/drying details and actual temperature-dependent measurements would be needed before assigning it to the raw-sludge case or expanding a thermodynamic domain. This lead advances an evidence gap; it does not by itself close the thermal model.
