# Bennamoun source availability and coupled-model implications

Reviewed 2026-09-09. Read-only investigation; no author contact, payment, access-gate bypass, guessed bitstream URL, or repository-code edit. These findings are based on the repository metadata and abstracts; neither Bennamoun full text was available through the public record.

## Bennamoun, Fraikin and Léonard 2014

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/07373937.2013.807281
- Primary institutional record: https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/159542
- Exact availability locator: Files / Full Text lists `DTJ7.pdf`, publisher postprint, 676.82 kB, with **Request a copy** only. This is not openly downloadable full-text evidence.
- Abstract paragraphs 1–2 describe air temperatures 80–200°C and velocities 1–2 m/s, camera observations of shrinking external dimensions, and infrared product temperature. The humidity range is missing in the rendered abstract.
- Abstract paragraphs 3–4 describe fitting the drying curve with a fourth-degree polynomial, then heat/mass balances, a Nusselt correlation, and radiative transfer. Thus the abstract supports thermal startup and evolving geometry as research directions, but does not establish an independently predictive moisture-transport law.
- No full-text parameter table, calibration split, uncertainty, geometry, numeric joint observations, or constitutive equation has been inspected. Do not register this record as a validated coupled material package or reuse its fitted coefficients as Wang2021 material properties.

## Bennamoun, Crine and Léonard 2013

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/07373937.2012.752743
- Primary institutional record: https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/147477
- Exact availability locator: Files / Full Text lists `dry_tech1.pdf`, publisher postprint, 966.62 kB, with **Request a copy** only.
- Abstract paragraph 1 describes two sludge origins, a cross-flow dryer, 1 kg wet extruded material, and 12 mm cylinders. Paragraphs 2–4 identify effective diffusion and convective mass-transfer coefficients by comparing Newton drying fits with a finite-cylinder diffusion solution. Shrinkage affects those inferred coefficients.
- This is evidence that geometry/origin alter inferred transport, not a demonstrated coupled temperature/moisture dataset available for extraction. The abstract contains an inconsistent unit on one convective mass-transfer coefficient (`m²/s` versus neighboring `m/s`), so numerical transcription is quarantined pending full text.
- The full paper, joint temperature observations, shrinkage measurement method, errors and coefficient estimation details remain unverified. Neither abstract can close the current material-evidence gap.

## Newly identified primary open alternative

- Nylen, Sheehan, Whelan and Dos Santos Antunes (2024), *Internal and surface temperature profiles of spherical biosolids particles during convective drying*, Drying Technology 42(13), 2044–2055.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/07373937.2024.2407959
- Primary repository: https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/83798/
- The record explicitly offers a published PDF under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Its abstract describes 44 trials, ten conditions, 88/109/138°C, gas velocities 1.6/2 m/s and 2/4 cm spherical particles from two defined wastewater-treatment/dewatering routes. Measured quantities include mass, center/internal temperature, surface temperature and shrinkage.
- This is a better candidate for simultaneously testing moisture, thermal startup and geometry than the inaccessible Bennamoun records. Applicability to a slab/brick requires an explicit geometry and material distinction.
- One direct PDF web fetch timed out; the repository HTML loaded successfully. No full-text extraction or assessment of numerical figure readability has been completed by this subtask. Root was informed for further independent work. Do not treat the abstract as an extracted validation dataset.
