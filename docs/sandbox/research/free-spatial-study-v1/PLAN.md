## Why original horizon cannot prove diffusion resolution

Using the case's nominal D=1e-8m²/s, dimensional sqrt(D*duration)=3.90625e-7m, only0.391 micrometers. Reference dx values are10000/5000/2500micrometers for2/4/8. The nominal diffusion Fourier number D*duration/dx² is1.53e-9/6.10e-9/2.44e-8. This is a characteristic scaling indicator, not a certified eigenvalue bound for the coupled multicomponent storage system. It shows the experiment is far too short and coarse to resolve a diffusion layer. A nonzero face flux at a step discontinuity can grow with refinement while integrated transport remains tiny; that must not be called converged diffusion. The initial two-parent step profile also precludes smooth-solution order assumptions near the interface.

At8 cells, dx²/D≈625s. Reaching even Fourier number.01 would require~6.25s, around409600 times the original horizon. Reaction A→B time constant~10s and tiny initial liquid inventory may hit depletion much earlier; mechanical/temperature/kinetic domains also matter. Naively extending the same 2^-17s step would be computationally infeasible. DO NOT increase D, smooth/change the initial profile or prescribe motion just to manufacture visible convergence under this case identity. A later longer-duration physical-case study needs explicit horizon/solver/adaptive budget, event-capable integration (run_service currently ordinary integrate), phase/domain feasibility and substantial cost reevaluation. Alternatively a separate exactly solvable dry transport benchmark can verify spatial discretization independently, but must carry its own case identity and cannot stand in for actual wet-case convergence.
